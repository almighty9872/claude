About This Site

katolikdunyasi.com was built to make the core texts and teaching of the Catholic faith accessible in Turkish: the Compendium of the Catechism, the process of becoming Catholic, a confession guide, the Mass, the parables of Jesus, prayers, a calendar of the saints and the twenty best-known among them, Catholic miracles, the roots of our faith in Anatolia, a section grounding the faith in reason, the Bible, frequently asked questions, and a directory of active Catholic churches in Turkey. This English section is a growing, partial mirror of that Turkish site; most of it is still Turkish-only for now, and pages are being translated over time.

The icon in the bottom-right corner of every page opens an accessibility menu, with ready-made profiles for motor impairment, blindness, color blindness and dyslexia, plus individual settings for contrast, text size, letter spacing and a screen-reader helper (details are on the Accessibility page).

Saint Peter the Apostle

Peter, whose original name was Simon, was an ordinary man who fished for a living on the shore of the Sea of Galilee. According to the Gospel of John, his brother Andrew introduces him to Jesus; Jesus looks at him and says, "Thou art Simon the son of Jona: thou shalt be called Cephas, which is interpreted Peter." In Matthew's Gospel this change of name becomes even clearer: in answer to Peter's confession, "Thou art Christ, the Son of the living God," Jesus says, "Thou art Peter; and upon this rock I will build my church." Catholic tradition takes this saying as the basis for Peter, and his successors the Popes, serving as the foundation of the Church's visible unity.

Falling and Rising

Peter is the boldest of the apostles, but also the most human. Trying to walk on water and sinking in fear; trying to keep Jesus from going to the cross and hearing the rebuke "Get thee behind me, Satan"; swearing at the Last Supper, "I will never deny thee," then denying he even knew Jesus three times before the cock crowed that same night: all of this makes him remarkably real. But after the Resurrection, on the shore of the Sea of Tiberias, Jesus asks him three times, "Lovest thou me?" and three times says, "Feed my lambs"; the threefold denial is answered with a threefold love.

Martyrdom in Rome

According to the Acts of the Apostles, Peter preaches the first sermon at Pentecost and thousands are baptized; he becomes the most visible leader of the early Church. Tradition takes him to Rome, where, during Emperor Nero's persecution of Christians, he is said to have asked to be crucified upside down, feeling unworthy to die in the same manner as his Lord. Today it is believed that a tomb directly beneath St. Peter's Basilica in the Vatican, confirmed by archaeological excavation, holds his remains. The Catholic Church considers every Pope the direct successor of Peter, which is why he is called "the first Pope." His feast day, shared with the apostle Paul, is June 29.
