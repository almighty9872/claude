About This Site

katolikdunyasi.com was built to make the core texts and teaching of the Catholic faith accessible in Turkish: the Compendium of the Catechism, the process of becoming Catholic, a confession guide, the Mass, the parables of Jesus, prayers, a calendar of the saints and the twenty best-known among them, Catholic miracles, the roots of our faith in Anatolia, a section grounding the faith in reason, the Bible, frequently asked questions, and a directory of active Catholic churches in Turkey. This English section is a growing, partial mirror of that Turkish site; most of it is still Turkish-only for now, and pages are being translated over time.

Sources and copyright

  • Catechism: Compendium of the Catechism of the Catholic Church, © 2005 Libreria Editrice Vaticana. The English pages of this site reproduce that original text; the Turkish pages carry a Turkish translation, © 2026 katolikdunyasi.com.
  • Saints: dates and ranks follow the General Roman Calendar; saints chosen for otherwise open days follow the Roman Martyrology. The biographies are original writing for this site.
  • The Parables of Jesus: based on the parables recounted in the Gospels (Matthew, Mark, Luke); the retellings and explanations are original writing for this site, not tied to a single translation.
  • Miracles: based on apparitions, relics and extraordinary events that have been examined and recorded by the Church; the text is original writing for this site.
  • Find a Church: the list of parishes in Turkey and their contact details are compiled from what each community publishes; Mass times may change.
  • The Becoming Catholic, Confession, Why We're Catholic, Christianity in Our Land, and FAQ sections are original writing for this site, grounded in the Catechism and reliable Catholic sources.
  • This site is not an official Church publication; it is a personal project.

Church of St. Mary

Latin Catholic
Currently closedRead more

This parish is currently closed. The building, shared by the Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant communities, was shut down by Bursa’s Directorate of Foundations, which found it unsafe in an earthquake; the last Mass was held on 16 February 2025. No alternative location has been assigned to the community and it is unclear when it will reopen. The Bursa Protestant community’s leader has said the stated reasons for the closure don’t match an independent geology report. We’d suggest checking the current status before visiting.

Where no Catholic church can be found: In some parts of Turkey there may be no Catholic church nearby, while an Orthodox one is. Canon law (1983 Code of Canon Law, Canon 844 §2) provides that when it is physically or morally impossible for a Catholic to reach a priest of their own Church, and a genuine need or spiritual advantage exists, and there is no danger of error or religious indifferentism, it is lawful to receive Holy Communion, Confession and the Anointing of the Sick from a Church (such as the Orthodox Church) whose sacraments the Catholic Church recognizes as valid. This is not an everyday practice but a permission for exceptional situations, and it does not extend to communities whose sacraments are not recognized as valid. The faithful who are properly disposed to receive Holy Communion (meaning not conscious of unconfessed mortal sin) may look for a local Orthodox church where they are; please keep in mind that each community has its own discipline, and a priest may not always be able to offer Communion.
