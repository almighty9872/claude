Patron of Lost Things, Doctor of the Church · 1195-1231

Saint Anthony of Padua

Born in Lisbon as Fernando (later Anthony), this young man first joins an Augustinian order, but in 1220, deeply moved by witnessing the bodies of five Franciscan missionaries martyred in Morocco being brought back to Portugal, he asks to transfer to the Franciscan order. On being accepted he changes his name to Anthony. He hopes to go to Morocco as a missionary, but falls gravely ill on the way; his ship is blown off course and drifts to Italy instead.

An Unexpected Preacher

Living in Italy as an unknown, ordinary friar, at an ordination ceremony it unexpectedly turns out that no one present is prepared to preach; at the last moment, Anthony is asked to speak. No one there knows how learned and compelling a speaker he is; his improvised sermon astonishes everyone. From that moment, Francis, the founder of the Franciscan order, appoints him to teach theology within the order, an unusual role for that early period of the order, since Francis himself was wary of academic titles.

Patron of Lost Things

Anthony sets out on an intense preaching tour across Italy and France; crowds are said to gather in squares too large for the churches to hold, just to hear him. Tradition recounts that once, when heretics refused to listen to him, he preached to fish on the seashore instead, and the fish raised their heads above the water to listen, a story that symbolizes the power of his words. After his death, following the story of a friar who recovered a lost psalter through prayer to him, he began to be venerated among the people as the patron of lost things; to this day, many Catholics worldwide, searching for something lost, pray, "Saint Anthony, Saint Anthony, please look around."

Anthony dies at thirty-five, exhausted from overwork. He is canonized only a year after his death, in 1232, one of the fastest such processes in Church history. He is declared a Doctor of the Church in 1946. His feast day is June 13.
